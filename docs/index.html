Sufficit

A query compiler for physics. Declare the model, the question, and the error you can tolerate. Get back a cheap computation and a proof of how wrong it could be.

Every rewrite here was built and proved by hand, one per problem. A planner chooses among competing rewrites by their declared cost, and the certificate decides whether the answer is good enough. A plan can also be built from stages, so one error budget is divided across several steps.

The illustrated tour. Start here.

Below, the certified cases, grouped by the field each one comes from. Every page states the idea, shows the program rendered from the module, and reports the result of running it, and each regenerates from a fresh run on every push.

The compiler itself

The machinery, shown on problems where the answer is already known.

  • The planner. One question asked at three tolerances gets three different algorithms. The certificates decide which answer is good enough, and the cost models only decide what to try first. The question is the ground energy of a quantum spin chain.
  • One budget, two bills. One error budget divided between two stages, resolution and statistics, before any data is bought. The question is a smeared spectral function from noisy correlator data, which is how lattice QCD asks about particle masses.

Quantum chemistry

Molecular energies, in a field that already argues in units of one kilocalorie per mole.

  • The hydrogen molecule. A two-sided bracket on the ground-state energy, ±10⁻¹³ hartree, from Gaussian integrals computed here from scratch.
  • The window-length ladder. Hydrogen chains far too long to diagonalise. A window of ℓ atoms costs 4ℓ, and the length of the chain never enters the cost.
  • A second lower bound on the same question. The two-particle density matrix, relaxed. The cost is polynomial in orbitals rather than exponential in states, so it still answers at 2²⁸ configurations.
  • A molecule too big to form. Polarisation functions double the orbital count and the Fock space is four to that power. Water in a published basis, and a six-atom cluster past the point where the matrix can be built.

Nuclear engineering

Whether a reactor core sustains a chain reaction.

  • A reactor is not symmetric. The criticality eigenvalue keff, which decides whether a chain reaction grows or dies, bracketed to 1 pcm. The operator is not self-adjoint, so positivity does the work a minimum principle cannot.

Semiconductor devices

What a device simulator reports, and what it has actually established.

  • A small residual is not a small error. The depletion charge of a pn junction, in the units a capacitance-voltage measurement reads. A stopped Newton iteration becomes a proof that a solution exists, and a radius that contains it.

Fusion and plasma physics

The equilibrium that confines a plasma, and the reductions fusion codes are built on.

  • A tokamak equilibrium with a guaranteed error bound. The magnetic flux that holds the plasma, solved by FEniCSx and certified by Prager-Synge. A realistic pressure profile puts that certificate out of reach, and an existence proof takes over.
  • The guiding-center hierarchy. Particle drift in a magnetic field, from the reduction gyrokinetics is built on. The exponent is proven and the constant is measured, which is what the asymptotic tier means.

Lattice field theory

Particle properties extracted from Euclidean correlators.

  • Two peaks from sixteen numbers. A smeared spectral density from sixteen correlator values. Sharper resolution costs certified error, and the certificate prices it.

Condensed matter and quantum dynamics

Spin systems at equilibrium, and after a kick.

  • A certified sweep across a phase transition. The ground energy of a spin chain at a hundred field strengths, from six exact solves, including the critical point.
  • A light cone, measured at its boundary. An observable after a quantum quench on a million-site chain, certified inside a Lieb-Robinson cone and refusing when the excitation front outruns the budget.
  • The 2D Ising model. Free energy and correlations from cluster expansions, carried in interval arithmetic, refusing outside the region where the expansion provably converges.

Fluid dynamics and coastal engineering

The load a wave puts on a structure.

  • A breaking wave against a sea wall. The impulse delivered to the wall certifies, the peak pressure refuses for want of an asymptotic range, and a berm provably takes the load to zero.

Turbulence and chaos

Long-time averages, over every trajectory at once.

  • A bound on chaos. The long-time average of a Lorenz coordinate, proven in exact rational arithmetic. The same method bounds mean quantities in turbulence, such as drag and heat transport.

Wave scattering and electromagnetics

Oscillatory kernels, and where compressing them starts to pay.

  • The butterfly crossover. Where a butterfly factorisation starts to beat plain low-rank compression, measured rather than asserted, with the certificate holding on both sides.

Gravitational-wave astronomy

Waveform templates, at the tolerance a detector can distinguish.

  • Waveform surrogates with stated odds. A waveform for any source parameter in about 0.3 ms, carrying a mismatch certificate matched to the detector's own resolution.

Molecular kinetics

Dropping the fast degrees of freedom, and pricing what that costs.

  • Dropping the fast variables. A ten-dimensional system reduced to its two slow coordinates, with the error priced by the fast sector's spectral gap. It refuses when there is no gap.
