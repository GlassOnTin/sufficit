Sufficit

A query compiler for physics. Declare the model, the question, and the error you can tolerate. Get back a cheap computation and a proof of how wrong it could be.

Every rewrite here was built and proved by hand, one per problem. A planner chooses among competing rewrites by their declared cost, and the certificate decides whether the answer is good enough. A plan can also be built from stages, so one error budget is divided across several steps. All four of the current plans work that way.

    • The window-length ladder. Hydrogen-chain brackets at cost 4ℓ. The chain length does not enter the cost.
    • A second lower bound on the same question. A relaxation of the two-particle density matrix, priced in orbitals rather than in states, and raced against the window ladder.
    • A molecule too big to form. Polarization functions double the orbital count, the Fock space is four to that power, and only one of the two rewrites survives the crossing.
    • A certified sweep across a phase transition. Six solves give a hundred certified points.
    • The butterfly crossover. Where the factorization starts to win, measured.
    • The 2D Ising model. Cluster expansions in interval arithmetic, refusing outside the region where they provably converge.
    • Two peaks from sixteen numbers. Smeared spectral functions, where sharper resolution costs certified error.
    • Dropping the fast variables. Mori-Zwanzig closures priced by the spectral gap.
    • A light cone, measured at its boundary. Quantum dynamics certified inside a cone, refusing when the cone outruns the budget.
    • The guiding-center hierarchy. The asymptotic tier: the exponent is proven and the constant is measured.
    • A bound on chaos. Lorenz time averages, proven over the rationals.
    • Waveform surrogates with stated odds. A conformal certificate serves the tolerance the detector can distinguish.
    • A breaking wave against a sea wall. The impulse certifies, the peak refuses, and a berm provably shields.
    • A tokamak equilibrium with a guaranteed error bound. FEniCSx proposes and Prager-Synge decides. A real pressure profile puts that certificate out of reach, and an existence proof takes over.
